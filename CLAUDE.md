# CLAUDE.md: Faith & Home Site Modeler

## Project
Interactive site modeler for the Faith & Home pattern book (Small Cities Lab, Lehigh University). Users compose church properties from simple pieces and see a chipboard-style study model. Full plan: docs/PLAN.md.

## Stack
- SvelteKit 3 (Svelte 5), TypeScript strict, adapter-node for Railway
- Threlte + three.js for the scene
- Railway Postgres with PostGIS, Drizzle ORM
- Directus (headless CMS, same Postgres instance, Railway service)
- Vitest for unit tests, Playwright for UI and screenshot tests

## Rules
- Site geometry is in meters in local coordinates; convert to lat/lng only at the edges (geocoding, saving origin).
- The `Site` object in `src/lib/modeler/types.ts` is the single source of truth. A site is the property (name, parcel, context, site type, front question, congregation profile) plus its existing conditions (`site.existing`, a `Plan`: the buildings as they stand, open spaces, trees) and its `plans`, the alternatives (may be empty). Rendering and metrics are derived from them; never store derived values.
- The site view is existing conditions, the only place the site and its buildings are edited. Plans inherit the site's buildings live and can only say what happens to each piece: to be removed (status `removed`), reuse zones, a different mix of uses (`planUses`), `addedStories` (new construction on top, drawn orange), a roof (`planRoof`). They can't move, reshape, cut or delete them (`store.isLocked`); what a plan draws is New. Open spaces and trees are copied when a plan is made, then the plan's own. In memory a plan holds the full list, so code reads `plan.structures` as before; saved, it holds only its own pieces plus `existingChoices` (`src/lib/modeler/existing.ts`: `expandPlan`, `compactPlan`, `syncPlan`, `storedSite`). Plans resync on every switch and save. Cuts and splits on the site carry each plan's choices to the pieces; deleting a site building with plan choices is confirmed first.
- The store edits one plan at a time: use `store.plan` (existing conditions on the site view, `store.onSite`), not `store.site`, for plan-level fields. Plan-only metrics take a `Plan`; metrics that need the parcel or site type take `(plan, site)`.
- Copy reactive state with the store's `copy()`, not `$state.snapshot(this.x)` alone: server builds (Vitest) compile that to the live value.
- Metrics and building-type inference are pure functions in `src/lib/modeler/metrics.ts` with tests.
- Land to sell: a plan can mark part of the property to be sold, drawn like an open space (an `OpenSpace` of kind `sale`; plans only, so it can't be drawn on the site view and `normalizeSite` drops it from existing conditions). It isn't open space. A building whose area-weighted center is on it is sold with the land (`soldStructures` in metrics.ts; its rooftop pieces too, not buildings to be removed), and so is an open space whose center is on it. The analysis, compare and the building type measure what the congregation keeps (`keptPlan`); lot coverage is the kept footprint over the parcel less the land to sell. Development type: reuse and new construction count on the land kept, and land to sell with neither is Downsize / sell. Drawn as a terracotta hatch with a dashed edge (over the buildings in plan view, so those sold read hatched); sold buildings are tinted terracotta in 3D and badged "sold" in the panel.
- Status is what physically stands: Existing, New, Removed. Adaptive reuse is not a status: it is a `ReuseZone` on an existing structure (an area stored normalized to its part, a floor range, a proposed use). Zones count toward their use and are carved out of the structure's slider program (its current use). A building's floors are one stack: its own stories, then any a plan adds to the whole building (`addedStories`), then a zone's own `addedStories` over its outline. A zone's floors can run up the whole stack and re-fit (never drop) when it shrinks; only its area on the building's own floors is reuse, the rest is new construction with its use (`zoneAreas` in metrics.ts), drawn orange with green edges. Old saves with the Reuse status convert to Existing plus a whole-building zone in `normalizeSite`. A plan can also change a standing building's mix of uses without drawing anything (`planUses`, `planUseWeights`, saved as a choice; `programOf` in metrics.ts reads the mix in effect): what a use gains over the site's share, on the building's own floors after its zones, is adaptive reuse by share (`shareReuse`), counted in reuse floor area, units and development type. Floors the plan adds take its mix as new construction. A mix equal to the site's clears itself.
- A building is a set of touching pieces: structures in the same group, or footprints within 0.5 m. Each placed or cut piece is its own structure; joined pieces share a `groupId`. Inference (`inferBuildingType`): 3+ buildings near each other is a Cluster; otherwise the main building decides: Object if the site's front question (`site.mainBuildingFront`) says it has no clear front or several, or if it has a triangle or circle piece; several pieces: Box-Plus; one piece: Box. A cluster stays a cluster whatever its main building is. A piece can sit on another's roof (`Structure.onRoofOf`: a drum, a rooftop apartment): a piece whose center lands on a building's footprint sits on its roof (the highest under it), rides that building's stack and moves with it, takes no lot coverage, joins its building, and is left out of the Object and Box-Plus rules. On the site it stands; in a plan it's New; the site's rooftop pieces go with their host when a plan removes it (`roofBase`, `settleRoofHosts` in geometry.ts). Thresholds are team-tunable constants; `Plan.buildingTypeOverride` holds the user's choice.
- One three.js scene with two cameras (orthographic plan, perspective orbit). Do not build a separate 2D editor. Compare draws two such canvases: each canvas is a `SceneView` (`scene/cameraRef.ts`: its element, scene, renderer and cameras; the editor's is `mainView`), passed by context from `Scene.svelte` to its cameras and `SceneRoot`. Code that needs a camera, the scene or the canvas takes the view (`hitTest`, `clientToSite`, `worldToScreen`, `topBelow`, `surfaceUnder`, `capturePlanImage`), defaulting to the editor's. `SceneRoot` draws a `plan` (the store's by default) and with `interactive={false}` draws no selection, handles or drawing.
- Compare (`CompareLayout.svelte`, replacing the editor while `store.compareWith` is set) is view state, never saved: B is the plan being viewed (`store.plan`), A is `store.compareA`, and the URL is the plan's own plus `?compare=<A's slug>` (empty for existing conditions), on edit and view links alike, so unsaved edits survive it. It's read-only. Both views share `store.planView` and fit both plans on entry; in 3D they orbit together (`OrbitSync`). What changed is pure (`planSummary`, `compareRows` in `src/lib/modeler/compare.ts`); the compare analysis (`CompareAnalysis.svelte`) shares its pieces with the single analysis (`PatternCard`, `ReportNotes`, `ActionSteps`, `UseAllocation`, the styles in `ui/report.css`, `patternFor` in match.ts).
- The 3D view is a model on a table in front of a studio backdrop (`COLORS.backdrop`, set as `scene.background` in 3D only, so exports carry it; plan view stays linen). The orbit stops short of level and never drops below whatever is under the camera (`cameraFloor` in view.ts, with `topBelow` in `scene/cameraRef.ts`). The wheel zooms in toward the surface under the cursor and the orbit pivot follows it; zooming out backs away from the pivot (`zoomAbout` in view.ts, wheel handling in `PerspCamera.svelte`, not OrbitControls' zoom), and the terrain's underside is drawn as cut board (`COLORS.cut`). Shadows are soft and a little light in 3D, as under a studio lamp (`shadow.radius`, `shadow.intensity` in `SceneRoot.svelte`); the board is lit enough that white foam stands off it, and the parcel mask is fainter there. Nothing is drawn beyond the board.
- Editor layout: the palette on the left; above the canvas a breadcrumb bar (`SiteHeader.svelte`: committee › site › plan ▾, the plan's name opening the plan menu, and a panel button); on the right a drawer that slides over the canvas without resizing it, with folder tabs on its edge (Spatial, Social, Notes; icons in `static/icons/`). `store.drawerOpen` and `store.sidebarTab` are view state, remembered in the browser. Selecting a building opens the drawer to Spatial, unless Notes is open.
- Notes are the pattern book's numbered dots, drawn in dusk blue (`--color-note`), since orange is new construction in the model (`Plan.annotations`, `{ n, x, y, text }`): each plan and existing conditions numbers its own from 1 in the order placed, and deleting renumbers; a new plan starts without the site's. They're placed, written and deleted in the Notes drawer, and dots show (and drag, in plan view) only while it's open (`store.showNotes`; `AnnotationDots.svelte`, an HTML overlay; in 3D they sit on whatever is under them). The analysis always shows them on its plan image and lists them; a PNG export draws them, with the list beneath, when the Notes drawer is open (`sceneExport.ts`). Thumbnails stay clean.
- Plan view framing (pan, zoom, fit) is `store.planView`, with pure math in `src/lib/modeler/view.ts`. It is view state: never saved in the Site. Threlte renders on demand, so code that changes a camera by hand must call `invalidate()`.
- No Google Maps or Google 3D Tiles. No raster map tiles inside the 3D model.
- Do not add dependencies without asking.
- Aesthetic is a physical study model (chipboard, basswood, white foam). Never aim for photorealism.
- Import app code with `#lib/...`. Env vars are declared in `src/env.ts` and read from `$app/env/private` (SvelteKit 3; not `$env/...`).
- Use pattern-book vocabulary in UI copy: box, box-plus, cluster, object; urban, suburban, rural; adaptive reuse, new construction.
- UI copy: concise, no em dashes.

## Patterns
- A site sits in one cell of the pattern matrix: building type × site type × development type (4 × 3 × 5 = 60 cells). Development type is per plan (`inferDevelopmentType` in metrics.ts, `Plan.developmentTypeOverride`): Existing conditions, Adaptive reuse, New development, Reuse + new, Downsize / sell. Selling shows in the model as land to sell; the override remains.
- The catalog lives in the database (`patterns`, `pattern_sections`, `pattern_variants`), edited in Directus (docs/DIRECTUS.md) and read on the server (`src/lib/server/patterns.ts`; row mapping in `pattern-rows.ts`). Types and axes stay in `src/lib/patterns/catalog.ts`; in the browser `getPatterns()` fetches `/api/patterns`. Each pattern covers a set of cells (`cells`); each cell should have one pattern: editors can break that in the CMS, so `/patterns/matrix` lists overlaps (`overlaps` in match.ts) and the first by `sort` wins. Social conditions don't add an axis: they pick a `Variant` within a pattern, the first whose every condition is answered and allowed. The placeholder patterns the team replaces were seeded by migration 0005 from `src/lib/patterns/seed.ts` (kept for tests).
- A pattern can show an example: a saved site's plan, or its existing conditions (`patterns.example_site_id`/`example_plan_id`). Its page shows the plan's thumbnail and a link to the read-only view. Until Directus runs, the analysis pegs the current plan ("Use this plan as the pattern's example", `PUT /api/patterns/[slug]/example`). Every save stores a ~480 px JPEG of what's on screen with the plan being viewed (`captureThumbnail` in sceneExport.ts; `plans.thumbnail`, or `sites.thumbnail` for existing conditions).
- Matching is pure (`src/lib/patterns/match.ts`): `siteCell`, `patternForCell`, `nearestPattern` (fewest differing axes; ties keep development type, then building type, then site type), `selectVariant`, `coverage`. Tested in `match.spec.ts`.
- The analysis always names the cell ("Urban · Box-Plus · Adaptive reuse"). Covered: the pattern and its variant. Not covered: "No pattern written for this combination yet" plus the closest written pattern and what differs. `/patterns/matrix` shows coverage; pattern pages show their cells (`MatrixGrid.svelte`).
- The four culture questions in the Social tab (partnering, capacity, change, role) are drafts for team review. Roles are any number (`CongregationProfile.role` is a list; one saved as a single answer loads as a list of one); a variant's role condition holds when any chosen role is allowed (`selectVariant`).

## Persistence
- `sites` holds each site as JSON in `data` (its settings and existing conditions) plus `name`; each plan is a row in `plans` (`data` is the stored plan: its own pieces and `existingChoices`), so the CMS can list and rename them. Reads assemble the two (`siteFromRows` in `src/lib/server/site-rows.ts`), falling back to `data.plans` for sites saved before the table; saves write both in one transaction and delete plan rows a site no longer has. Plan ids are uuids (`planUuid` turns older ids into stable ones). Server helpers in `src/lib/server/sites.ts`. Directus shares the database; drizzle ignores its `directus_*` tables.
- Every read and write passes through `normalizeSite` (`src/lib/modeler/serialize.ts`), and every write stores `storedSite(site)`. When adding a field to `Site`, update it there with a default so older saves still load. Sites saved before the site view take existing conditions from their first plan; a first plan named Existing conditions that proposes nothing becomes the site view.
- Schema changes: edit `schema.ts`, then `npm run db:generate` and `npm run db:migrate` (reads `DATABASE_URL` from `.env`).
- URLs are `/modeler/<site>[/<plan>][/view]` (`src/lib/modeler/slug.ts`); `/modeler/<site>` is the site view (existing conditions, no slug), and an unknown plan slug goes there. Site slugs are unique across sites (`site_slugs` table, assigned by the server); plan slugs are unique within a site (stored in the plan, assigned by `assignPlanSlugs` on save). Collisions get `-2`, `-3`. Old slugs are kept, so renames redirect. Reserved: site `sites`, `new`, `view`; plan `view`. The API (`/api/sites/[id]`) uses the uuid.
- Plan switches and renames update the URL with a shallow `goto` (`syncUrl` in `SiteBar.svelte`), so unsaved edits survive.
- Accounts and committees: people sign in by email link (no passwords; `src/lib/server/auth.ts`, sessions in the `fh_session` cookie, every token stored only as a SHA-256 hash; emails via Resend, `src/lib/server/email.ts`, printed to the server log while `RESEND_API_KEY` is unset). Email links open a page with a button (`/auth/verify`, `/auth/invite`); only pressing it (a POST) uses the link, so mail scanners that open links can't spend them. Sign-in requests are limited: 3 live links per address, 10 requests per network address per 15 minutes (`rate-limit.ts`). `hooks.server.ts` puts the user and their committees in `locals.user`; the root layout passes it to every page.
- Committees own sites (`sites.committee_id`). Members edit them, leads also invite and remove members, change roles and delete sites; a committee keeps at least one lead. The Faith & Home team committee (`is_team`) may do anything anywhere and alone pegs pattern examples. The rules are pure functions in `src/lib/server/access.ts` (tested); the server checks them on every load and API call, the UI only hides what isn't allowed. Slugs are guessable, so private sites (`visibility: 'members'`) read as not found to others and ask signed-out visitors to sign in; public sites keep an open `.../view` link. The editor needs edit rights (viewers are sent to the view link); `store.readOnly` and `store.rights` gate the UI.
- `/modeler` is open to try. Saving needs sign-in: signed out, the sketch is kept in `sessionStorage` while you sign in, then saved (`/modeler?restore=1`). A new site goes to the user's only committee, or they choose one, or start one (while the team allows: `settings.allow_new_committees`, toggled in `/admin`). `/committees` lists your committees (the team sees every one) and starts new ones; the team can also create a committee for someone else and invite its first lead from `/admin`. Committee pages (`/committees/<slug>`; slugs come from the name, unique across committees, and a rename keeps old slugs as redirects, as do ids) manage members, invitations, the name and an optional one-line description. An invitation can carry a note (up to 500 characters), quoted in the email under the inviter's name and not stored (`inviteEmail` in `src/lib/server/invite-email.ts`).
- Saves carry the site's `version`: a save made from an older version is refused (409) and the bar offers Reload. No real-time co-editing.
- Playwright tests write to the `.env` database and delete the rows they create. They run signed in as a Faith & Home team member (`tests/e2e/global-setup.ts`; sessions made directly in the database by `tests/e2e/auth.ts`, which also makes other users and committees for access tests). Mutating API calls from Playwright's `request` need an `origin` header or SvelteKit returns 403.

## Address mode
- Address data is fetched once at setup (`/api/context`) and saved into the Site: `context` (foam buildings, roads, sources), `origin`, `parcel`. Nothing re-fetches on load.
- Sources, terms, attribution and upkeep are in docs/DATA_SOURCES.md; update it when a source is added, dropped or bumped. Prefer global sources; parcels are a per-county registry with drawing as the fallback.
- Pure geometry is in `src/lib/modeler/geo.ts` (projection, footprint fitting, heights from tags, site type). Site coordinates: x east, y south (north is up in plan view).
- Real footprints become `rect` parts when nearly rectangular, otherwise `poly` parts with points normalized to w×d.
- "Draw outline" draws a structure point by point (always a `poly`, `outlinePart` in geo.ts). Poly pieces show point handles in plan view (drag a point, drag an edge's midpoint to add one, double-click to remove; at least 3) instead of corner resize handles; "Edit points" turns a rect, square or triangle into one; "Make rectangle" turns an outline into its best-fitting rectangle where it stands, at its own angle (`minAreaRect`), so it can take a gable roof. Rotating by the handle lines up with nearby walls (other pieces within 40 m and the parcel, any quarter turn, within 4°: `snapRotation` in snap.ts) or turns in 15° steps; Shift turns freely; the angle shows while turning. Drawn and dragged points snap to other structures' corners, then edges, and the parcel, within about 10 px (`snapPoint`; Alt places freely). A piece's reuse zones keep their ground when its points move, clipped to the new outline, re-clipped from the drag's start so they never erode.
- The board is a square around the origin, half-width `context.halfSize` (150 m default). The origin starts at the address point; when a parcel is set whose center is more than 40 m off it (a campus whose address is at the street), the origin moves to the parcel's center, every position shifts with it so nothing moves on the ground, and the neighborhood and terrain reload around it, no smaller than the board was (`src/lib/modeler/recenter.ts`: `recenterOffset`, `shiftSite`, `reloadedBuildings`). Sites set before that offer "Center the board on the parcel" in the address panel (`store.centerBoard`). Undo restores the old origin and board. Buildings added from the map leave the foam, matched by where they stand (`foamOff` in geo.ts), never by exact coordinates, since a moved or reloaded board's polygons differ by a hair; a reload never brings back foam on the site's buildings. A parcel within 30 m of the edge, or tall enough that fitted on a wide (2.5:1) screen the view would pass the board, reloads the context on a bigger board (`boardHalfFor` in geo.ts, max 400 m). "Widen the board" (in the address panel, and in the banner while tracing a lot) loads a bigger one by hand until the largest: the size a parcel needs, else the next step, 150, 250, 400 m (`widerBoard` in geo.ts; `store.canWidenBoard`, `widenBoard`), then shows the whole board. It's for a property that runs past the edge where there's no parcel data to size the board from (an address can land far from the buildings on a big campus), and sites saved on a board too small for a wide screen. Pan and zoom stop at the board's edge on the canvas's wider side, so no linen shows past it (`clampView` in view.ts; `store.boardHalf`; grid mode uses the 400 m ground).
- Credit data wherever it appears: the map's credits button, the analysis line and PNG exports, all from `src/lib/modeler/credits.ts`, which credits only the sources a site uses (`context.sources`, `context.buildingSources`, `context.terrain`). A new source needs a credit there.
- Terrain: `context.terrain` is a 5 m elevation grid from the AWS Terrain Tiles (global, 2017; `terrain.regions` drives the national credits), fetched by `/api/terrain` apart from `/api/context` so setup never waits on it; older sites get "Add terrain". The surface, contours and draping are derived in `src/lib/modeler/terrain.ts`, never stored: `store.terrain` is the natural surface (the origin's ground is y = 0), `store.ground` its 3D mesh. The ground meets buildings flush: a building's floors start at the highest ground under it (full data height uphill) and a plinth in its color reaches the lowest (`buildingFooting`), so nothing floats or sinks; foam the same. Only 3D sets things on the terrain; trees sit on the surface and flat things are draped a few centimeters up (`scene/drape.ts`). Plan view prints natural contours and editing stays flat. The Terrain button turns it all off for a flat board (`store.showTerrain`, view state, not saved).
- e2e mocks our own `/api/geocode*`, `/api/context` and `/api/terrain`; parsers are tested against fixtures in `src/lib/server/fixtures/` captured live.

## Colors
Pull from the pattern book: green (adaptive reuse), orange (new construction), blue, red, off-white base. Define as CSS tokens and share with three.js materials.
- In 3D, buildings and roofs carry a faint paper grain (15%) and the board and terrain a fainter one (7%), from a CC0 kraft-paper texture laid by world position (`scene/grain.ts`, `applyGrain` via `oncreate`). Foam, draped print and plan view stay flat.

## Deploying
- Railway, per docs/DEPLOY.md: `railway.json` builds, runs `npm run db:migrate:prod` (`scripts/migrate.js`, drizzle-orm's migrator) before each deploy, and starts `node build`. Production has its own database; `.env` is development only and never points at production. adapter-node behind Railway needs `ORIGIN`, `ADDRESS_HEADER`/`XFF_DEPTH` (the rate limit) and `BODY_SIZE_LIMIT=4M` (large saves).
- People set the name their committees see at `/account`.

## Workflow
- Start each milestone in plan mode and wait for approval.
- Small commits; run `npm run check`, `npm test`, and Playwright before declaring done.
- For visual changes, save screenshots to `tests/screenshots/` and summarize what changed.
- Update this file when a convention changes.
